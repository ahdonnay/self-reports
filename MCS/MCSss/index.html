CATSS

Changes in Awareness and Tolerance of Sensory Stimuli

v.2026.10.13.h

About this checklist

CATSS records changes in two different responses to sensory stimuli since a prior date or event that you specify. Awareness means the level of a stimulus you notice. Tolerance means the maximum level of that stimulus you can tolerate. A change in one does not necessarily mean the same change in the other.

How to complete CATSS

For both Awareness and Tolerance, choose one response for each stimulus:

  • N No change
  • Up Up
  • Dn Down
  • UD Up then Down = first abnormally up, then abnormally down at least once during the time period
  • DU Down then Up = first abnormally down, then abnormally up at least once during the time period

Every response starts at N. If you are unsure whether your awareness or tolerance changed, leave it at N. “Up” and “down” describe the direction of change, not whether the change was good or bad. The dashboard updates automatically, and your answers are saved in this browser on this device.

Important distinction: Greater awareness can occur with greater, unchanged, or lower tolerance. Record each column independently.

Clinical note: CATSS is a structured self-report, not a diagnostic test. Discuss important or sudden sensory changes with a qualified clinician.

Privacy & your data

Your answers are stored only in this web browser, on this device. Nothing is sent to any server. Anyone who uses this browser profile can see your answers, so on a shared or public computer use Clear answers when you finish. To move your report to another device, use your browser's Print → Save as PDF.

Your information and comparison point

All Awareness and Tolerance responses compare your current experience with this same prior date or event.

Enter your name for a clinician-facing report, or use a private codename if you prefer.

Fills automatically after the first change is recorded; you can change it.
